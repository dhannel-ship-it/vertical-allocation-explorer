Sources and limits

  • Elevate_Dual_Mandate_Outcome_Model.xlsx (internal): $14M fund, 25% ED / 75% VC, 9 ED and 27 VC companies, $1M per-company cap, ED and VC Worst/Base/Best tables. The 25/50/25 regime weights are an added assumption.
  • Correlation Ventures via Seth Levine: ~21,000 US financings 2004–2013, 65% returned < 1x, 10% ≥ 5x, 4% ≥ 10x. The all-sector anchor.
  • Bruce Booth (Forbes, 2014): BioPharma loss rate 57% vs 66% for other sectors; > 5x 11.5% vs 9.8%; 50x+ much rarer in BioPharma.
  • LifeSciVC (2016), Cambridge Associates data: 5x+ deals 15% in biopharma vs 10% in IT/software.
  • Adams Street via Booth (2012): dollar-weighted loss ~55% overall, biotech ~36%, internet ~59%.
  • Horsley Bridge via a16z: ~6% of deals produced ~60% of returns. Industry Ventures: early-stage loss ~65%, median fund MOIC 1.9x.
  • MIT Energy Initiative (2016) and CTVC: cleantech 2006–11 lost over half of $25B; climate exits take ~9 years.
  • GFI via Green Queen: alt-protein funding fell to $881M in 2025 from $6.9B in 2021; 40+ ventures shut, sold or went bankrupt in one year. CropLife: 54 agtech exits in 2025, all M&A, no IPOs.
  • Russ Fein: IonQ bought Oxford Ionics for $1.075B. PostQuantum: the 2021 quantum SPACs delivered ~12 cents per dollar of projected 2025 revenue, and several fell 84–90%+.
  • Carta (2025): seed-to-A graduation down in every sector since 2021; hardware held up best.

Rows tagged Judgment have no published bucket data behind them. Check sizes come from the stage averages and conviction ranges. Each VC company lands in one next-round scenario (up, flat or down round, a Series B Elevate cannot join, or failure) that shifts its outcome odds; Elevate may follow on once in up, flat and down rounds at that round's price, and skipping a round can cost part of the initial stake. There is no entry-valuation or ownership term, conviction changes size but not odds, and correlates deals only through exit-multiple shocks. Fund TVPI = fund gross multiple × invested share, less carry on gains above 1x; at the defaults (100%, 0%) it equals gross, matching Elevate's workbook. Indirect returns such as jobs or impact are not counted. Theoretical model; not investment advice.
